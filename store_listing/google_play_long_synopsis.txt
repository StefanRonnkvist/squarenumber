Square Number is a focused falling-number puzzle where every placement matters. Guide each active square across a compact five-column board, create matches, and keep the stack below the top border.

Clear the board in two ways:
- Connect 2 or more equal squares at an edge or corner.
- Build an orthogonally connected chain of 3 or more unique consecutive values. Chains can turn corners, but values cannot repeat.

Each matching group scores (sum of its values) x (number of squares in that group); group scores are added together for each clear. When a clear makes the stack fall into another match, an increasing cascade multiplier applies until the next square appears. Floating score markers show the points earned for each clear.

The pressure rises with your score. Starting from your chosen base speed, effective fall speed increases by 0.10x every 500 points, up to 1.50x, turning careful setup into quick decisions as each run develops.

Choose a base speed from 0.20x to 1.50x and a generated number range, then play in system, light, or dark theme. Drag the active square sideways or down; releasing it snaps it to one of five columns, and placed squares cannot be moved. Pause when you need a break, or start a fresh run at any time. The adaptive interface supports phones, tablets, and desktop screens; phone gameplay uses portrait orientation, while tablet and desktop support portrait and landscape.

Square Number saves a non-empty board when the app moves to the background and restores it in a paused state on your next launch. Your highest score and 10 best completed runs stay on your device, while a positive current score appears in Score History as Current only when it qualifies for the top 10. Settings includes Controls, Score History, Information, and Help. Information provides a feedback form with app diagnostics and a viewer for hosted submissions. Sending feedback or loading hosted submissions requires an internet connection; game progress and scores are stored locally, and no account is required.

Return to the same stack, chase a new best, and look for the cascade that changes the run.
